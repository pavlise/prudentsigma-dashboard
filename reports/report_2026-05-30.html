Dominant Macro Theme Today:
The headline story of the week is oil's historic May collapse — WTI
crude is on pace for a -17% monthly decline, the worst since 2020,
driven by reports of a preliminary US-Iran ceasefire deal that could
reopen the Strait of Hormuz and restore constrained global supply.
This deflationary energy shock is simultaneously a tailwind for
equities (lower input costs, suppressed inflation) and a sharp
headwind for energy stocks, creating a pronounced bifurcation. The
Fed remains in wait-and-see mode at 3.50–3.75%, watching whether the
energy-driven disinflation provides political cover to resume cuts.

SECTION 2 — TECHNICAL SNAPSHOT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Technical Condition Summary:

- Trend structure: SPX and NDX are in clean bull structures —
  above SMA20, SMA50, and SMA200 — and both printed 52-week highs.
  NDX's 13% premium to SMA50 is aggressive even by bull-market
  standards. Contrast this with BTC, which sits below all three
  moving averages in a confirmed bear structure. Gold and Silver are
  in a middle ground: above SMA200 (long-term uptrend intact) but
  below SMA20/50, signaling a corrective consolidation within a
  secular bull. WTI is below SMA20/50 but elevated above SMA200 —
  the Iran ceasefire has cracked the supply-shock premium.

- Momentum: NDX at RSI 77 and SPX at RSI 74 are in overbought
  territory — historically, RSI above 70 on monthly timeframes often
  precedes 5–10% corrections even in bull markets. Critically, SPX
  shows a bearish MACD divergence (price at ATH, MACD rolling
  over) — a classic warning signal. BTC (RSI 35), ETH (RSI 32), and
  WTI (RSI 38) are near or in oversold territory; WTI's BB% at 0.05
  puts it at the very bottom of its Bollinger envelope, suggesting a
  technical bounce is possible even if the fundamental trend is lower.

- Volatility: VIX at 15.32 is subdued, but the VIX term
  structure (spot 15.32 vs VIX3M 18.66) is in normal contango —
  indicating the market is not complacent about medium-term risk.
  ATR% is highest in WTI (7.10%) and Silver (3.94%), making
  position sizing critical in those markets. SPX's ATR% of 0.91%
  is low, consistent with the low-VIX environment.

- Cross-asset signals: The SPX/HYG correlation of 0.85 is
  confirming — high-yield credit is rallying alongside equities,
  removing one key bear flag. However, SPX/10Y at -0.84 is a
  double-edged sword: the recent 5D rally in bonds (yields -2.30%)
  is supportive now, but any reversal toward 4.67 (52W high) would
  pressure equities. Gold/DXY at -0.66 confirms gold is being
  dollar-driven — with DXY flat/consolidating near SMA50, Gold
  needs a fresh dollar catalyst to resume its uptrend.

- Options sentiment: SPY P/C volume ratio of 0.95 (Neutral) and
  QQQ P/C volume of 0.82 (Neutral) suggest no strong directional
  hedging by options markets. However, SPY P/C open interest at 1.66
  indicates significant structural put positioning — institutions
  have tail hedges in place even as VIX is low.

Sector Rotation: Tech (XLK) is the undisputed leader: +5.89% 5D
and +19.76% 1M vs SPX +5.15% 1M — a +14.6% relative outperformance
this month. This is AI-cycle momentum compounding on top of Q1
earnings beats across mega-cap tech. Every other sector is
underperforming SPX on a 1-month basis: Energy (XLE -10.78% rel)
and Utilities (XLU -10.33% rel) are the deepest laggards. Financials
(-6.20% rel) and Staples (-6.81% rel) show defensive money is being
ignored. The signal: this is a narrow, momentum-driven rally in which
real money is rotating violently out of energy and defensives into
growth/tech. Narrow breadth is historically a late-cycle warning.

HIGHEST IMPACT EVENT — May Non-Farm Payrolls (Friday, June 5):

The May jobs report is the pivotal event of the week. With Q1 GDP
revised alongside PCE data released May 28, the Fed's attention now
turns squarely to the labor market. Consensus expects approximately
160–180K payrolls added (down from prior months) with unemployment
holding at 4.2%. The asymmetric risk is significant:

  - Upside surprise (>200K): Bond sell-off, yields back toward
    4.60–4.67%, dollar strength, equity pullback as rate-cut
    expectations are pushed out — bearish for Gold and growth
    stocks in the near term.

  - Downside miss (<130K): Bond rally, yields toward 4.20–4.30%,
    Fed cut expectations repriced for July, equity bulls and Gold
    bulls win. The Iran disinflation narrative + weak jobs = Fed
    dovish pivot catalyst.

  - In-line print (150–180K): Status quo — markets likely drift,
    tech momentum continues.

Given oil's historic collapse and possible ceasefire-driven demand
destruction in the energy sector, a weaker employment reading in
energy-adjacent states is a plausible tail. Watch ADP Wednesday for
a preview signal.
