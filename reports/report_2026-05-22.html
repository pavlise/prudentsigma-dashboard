Dominant Macro Theme Today:
The Iran war ceasefire remains the single biggest macro driver of 2026. After weeks of "life support" headlines and intermittent fighting since May 4, both Washington and Tehran signaled renewed progress in Pakistan-mediated talks overnight — sending equities, oil, and risk assets sharply higher. Markets are trading a peace premium, but the fundamental impasse over uranium enrichment limits and Strait of Hormuz tolls remains unresolved. With only ~5% of normal shipping traffic navigating the Strait and oil near $100/bbl, any headline reversal could violently re-price the risk-off scenario.

A secondary but important theme: new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh takes his oath today, inheriting a difficult mandate — FOMC minutes from May 20 confirmed that most policymakers remain open to additional hikes if inflation stays sticky above 2%, even as consumer sentiment collapsed to a record-low 44.8 (UMich May final revision). The macro backdrop is stagflationary with a geopolitical overlay.

Technical Condition Summary:

- Trend structure: SPX is clawing back from the sharp geopolitical-driven selloff earlier in May, now attempting to reclaim the 5,900 area. The Dow's surge to 50,724 is constructive, but Nasdaq's relative underperformance (+0.47% vs Dow's +0.87%) suggests continued rotation away from growth/tech into cyclicals and energy. The S&P's 200-SMA (~5,750 estimated) is providing a floor but is not yet decisively recaptured — the price action remains corrective, not impulsive.

- Momentum: Gold's -0.55% decline on a risk-on session is notable — it typically holds or advances when oil/geopolitical risk surges. This divergence could signal that the gold rally is momentarily exhausted near $4,517. Bitcoin's -0.76% on an equity up-day suggests crypto is decoupling from the risk-on narrative — watch $75,000 as the line-in-the-sand.

- Volatility: VIX appears elevated (estimated 22–24 range) given ongoing geopolitical uncertainty, though it should be drifting lower on ceasefire optimism. This is not a complacent market — elevated VIX reflects genuine macro uncertainty. WTI ATR at these price levels (~$98) is unusually wide, creating large intraday swings.

- Cross-asset signals: The DXY remaining "rangebound" despite elevated yields (4.57%) and hawkish FOMC minutes is a bearish dollar signal — the flight-to-safety USD bid is being offset by the Iran peace dividend. This DXY softness is structurally supportive for gold, commodities, and EM assets. Watch for a DXY break above 104.5 as the signal that safe-haven USD demand is reasserting.

- Options sentiment: With Iran ceasefire trades actively unwinding hedges, put/call ratios across major indices should be declining. This short-squeeze dynamic in equities explains the Dow's outperformance. However, tail risk hedges for oil remain well-bid given the fragility of the ceasefire.

Sector Rotation: Energy is the clear week's leader — WTI at $98 after surging from mid-$80s on re-escalation is driving massive outperformance for E&Ps and integrated majors. Cyclicals (industrials, materials) are next as the ceasefire trade re-opens global supply chains. Tech is lagging — rising real yields and the hawkish FOMC lean compress growth stock multiples. Defensive sectors (utilities, staples) are underperforming as the risk-on rotation unfolds.
