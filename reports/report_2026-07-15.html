Markets are caught between a geopolitical oil shock and a still-undecided Fed. Equities are shrugging off both risks so far — SPX printed a fresh closing high — but the VIX term structure in contango and resilient credit spreads suggest complacency rather than resolution.

SECTION 2 — TECHNICAL SNAPSHOT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Technical Condition Summary:
- Trend structure: SPX is in a clean bull structure — above SMA20/50/200 with the 200D nearly 8% below spot, confirming an intact uptrend. NDX is more fragile: it's below its SMA20 (29,586 vs 29,689) despite being above SMA50/200 — a short-term stall inside a longer-term uptrend, likely tech consolidating after its recent AI-earnings-driven run. WTI is the standout: above SMA20 and SMA200 but still below SMA50 (86.33), meaning the blockade rally has reversed a multi-week downtrend but hasn't yet erased it.
- Momentum: RSI readings are unremarkable across major indices (SPX 57, NDX 51) — no overbought equity signal despite the highs. The commodity complex shows a bearish/bullish RSI divergence: gold (40) and silver (38) are rolling toward oversold while WTI (54) and crypto (BTC 55, ETH 63) show renewed bullish momentum. MACD confirms this split — bullish on SPX, WTI, BTC, ETH; bearish on NDX, DXY, gold.
- Volatility: VIX at 16.50 sits well off its 52W high (31.05) and the term structure remains in normal contango (VIX3M 19.30 > VIX), meaning options markets are not pricing near-term equity stress from the Hormuz situation. By contrast, ATR% shows WTI (4.88%) and silver (3.71%) carrying multiples of SPX's realized volatility (1.10%) — the vol is concentrated in commodities, not equities.
- Cross-asset signals: SPX/HYG correlation of +0.84 with HYG price stable (79.68, +0.14% 1M) indicates credit is not yet flagging equity risk — a genuine risk-off would show HYG cracking while SPX holds. Gold/DXY correlation is -0.52 (moderately negative), meaning gold's slide is only partly dollar-driven; the rest reflects reduced safe-haven demand even amid a live Middle East conflict, an unusual and worth-watching divergence. SPX/VIX at -0.89 is behaving normally (fear gauge intact, no structural break).
- Options sentiment: SPY P/C at 0.89 and QQQ P/C at 1.16 are both neutral — no crowded directional bet in either index, consistent with a market that hasn't decided whether the Hormuz shock or the soft CPI print matters more.

Sector Rotation: Financials (XLF, +5.69% 1M, +4.18% relative to SPX) are the clear 1-month leader, powered by the Goldman Sachs beat (EPS $20.98 vs. $14.48 est.) and broader bank earnings strength — a classic pro-cyclical, rate-normalization rotation. Healthcare (XLV, +3.37% 1M) and Utilities (XLU, +3.26% 1M) are also outperforming, which is a more defensive signal sitting oddly alongside financials' leadership. Laggards are Staples (XLP, -2.12% 1M) and Real Estate (XLRE, -1.08% 1M) — both rate-sensitive/defensive plays being sold, not bought, which argues against a "flight to safety" reading despite the Hormuz headlines. Net signal: institutional flow is rotating into cyclicals/earnings-winners (financials) rather than into classic geopolitical hedges, reinforcing the medium-conviction "equities not yet pricing the shock" thesis.
