Technical Condition Summary:
- Trend structure: SPX and NDX remain in intact bull structure above their 200-day MAs (+6.3% and +7.3% respectively) but have slipped below their 20-day averages — a short-term pullback inside a longer uptrend, not a trend break. WTI is the standout: above all three MAs with a +29.2% premium to its 200-day, the steepest trend extension on the board.
- Momentum: RSI is broadly neutral across equities and metals (46-47), signaling indecision rather than exhaustion. The exception is WTI at RSI 75 (overbought) — a crowded long vulnerable to any de-escalation headline. MACD is bearish on SPX, NDX, Gold, Silver, and BTC simultaneously, a rare cluster that argues for defensive positioning into the FOMC print.
- Volatility: VIX (17.10) sits in contango against VIX3M (19.28) — the market is pricing *future* stress higher than current stress, consistent with FOMC positioning rather than an active shock. ATR% is elevated in WTI (3.91%) and Silver (2.88%), confirming both are the most reactive assets on the board right now.
- Cross-asset signals: The correlation matrix confirms the macro story — WTI is negatively correlated to SPX (-0.68), 10Y yields (0.71 positive to WTI, meaning rate pressure and oil pressure are moving together), and HYG (-0.79). Credit (HYG) is the cleanest read-through: it's negatively correlated to both yields and oil, so further strength in either is a headwind for spreads. Gold's negative correlation to DXY (-0.62) explains why gold is *falling* despite Middle East risk — dollar strength on hike odds is dominating the safe-haven bid.
- Options sentiment: SPY (1.53) and QQQ (1.55) put/call ratios both show elevated put buying — institutional hedging is already underway ahead of the Fed decision, consistent with the bearish MACD cluster.

Sector Rotation: Energy (XLE) is the clear leader, +6.36% relative to SPX over the past month on the oil spike, while Industrials (XLI) is the worst laggard at -6.76% relative — squeezed by rising input costs and higher discount rates. Healthcare (XLV, +2.36% rel) is picking up defensive rotation flows, while Utilities (XLU, -3.49% rel) is being sold despite its usual defensive profile, likely on rate-hike duration risk. This is a classic late-cycle, inflation-shock rotation: energy up, rate-sensitive cyclicals and duration-sensitive defensives down together.

HIGHEST IMPACT EVENT: The September 16 FOMC decision is the fulcrum for the week. With futures pricing ~85% odds of a 25bp hike on the back of above-target CPI/PPI and a resilient labor market, the *consensus* outcome is already substantially priced into DXY strength and the equity put/call skew. The asymmetric risk is a dovish surprise (hold, or a hike paired with dovish guidance citing oil-driven "transitory" inflation) — that scenario would trigger a sharp unwind of crowded USD longs and equity hedges, likely producing an outsized SPX/NDX relief rally and a DXY/yield reversal. Conversely, a hike *plus* hawkish forward guidance (further tightening flagged) would compound the existing equity and credit pressure, especially given HYG's negative correlation to yields (-0.80). Position sizing into the decision should account for this binary skew rather than the base case alone.
