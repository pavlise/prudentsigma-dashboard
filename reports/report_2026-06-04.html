SECTION 2 — TECHNICAL SNAPSHOT
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Trend Structure:
The broad equity complex maintains a constructive bull structure — SPX trades
+10.6% above its SMA200 and +6.2% above SMA50; NDX is even more extended at
+19.1% above SMA200. Critically, however, both post bearish MACD readings, a
signal that momentum is rolling over even as price holds elevated. In contrast,
Gold, Silver, and WTI all sit BELOW both SMA20 and SMA50 despite being above
SMA200 — a corrective/consolidation phase within longer-term bull structures.
BTC is the lone outright bear: below all three SMAs, with a deeply negative
SMA spread (-19.4% vs SMA200).

Momentum:
The momentum picture is bifurcated. NDX RSI at 73 is firmly overbought — a
zone where prior corrections have averaged -8% to -12% over 3-4 weeks when
accompanied by MACD divergence. BTC and ETH sit at RSI 18 — an extreme
oversold reading that historically produces mean-reversion bounces of 15-25%,
though structural bears require more than a single oversold reading to reverse.
SPX at RSI 69 is approaching but not yet at overbought (80+); this provides a
narrow window before a more consequential reversal signal triggers.

Volatility:
VIX at 15.55 remains subdued, but the term structure tells a different story:
VIX3M at 19.36 creates a steep contango spread of 3.81 points — the options
market is pricing significantly more volatility over the next 90 days than the
next 30. This is consistent with event risk from NFP (Jun 5), CPI (Jun 10),
PPI (Jun 11), and FOMC (Jun 17). ATR% highlights WTI (6.24%) and Silver
(3.63%) as the most volatile assets by normalized range — both are coiled.

Cross-Asset Signals:
The 30-day correlation matrix reveals a critical warning: SPX/10Y at -0.84 means
rising yields remain the primary equity headwind — and with 10Y at 4.47% and
trending toward the 4.67% 52-week high, the directional pressure is clear. The
DXY/Gold correlation at -0.70 confirms that recent Gold softness (-3.71% in 1M)
is dollar-driven rather than pure risk-on selling. The SPX/HYG spread remains
healthy at +0.84 correlation — credit markets are not yet flashing stress,
which prevents a more bearish interpretation of equity conditions.

Options Sentiment:
SPY P/C volume at 0.84 and QQQ P/C volume at 0.94 both read neutral — neither
fear-driven put accumulation nor complacent call chasing. However, QQQ OI P/C
at 3.11 is notably elevated, suggesting longer-dated hedging is already in place
for NDX downside. This is structurally protective but not yet signaling acute
stress.

Sector Rotation:
Tech (XLK) is the unambiguous leader at +10.48% relative to SPX over 1M —
institutional flows are concentrated in AI-driven mega-cap names. Energy (XLE)
is the second-best performer (+0.51% relative, 1M), lifted by the Middle East
bid. The losers are rate-sensitive: Utilities (XLU, -7.15% relative), Real
Estate (XLRE, -4.84%), and Staples (XLP, -4.97%). This is a classic
late-cycle rotation pattern — cyclicals and tech lead, defensives lag — but
notably, Financials (XLF) are also underperforming (-2.20% relative 1M),
suggesting rate-hike fears are cutting both ways.

HIGHEST IMPACT EVENT — May NFP (Friday, June 5, 08:30 ET):
This is the final major labor print before the June 17 FOMC decision and
carries outsized binary risk. With April CPI at 3.8% — well above the 2%
target — a hot NFP print (>200K jobs, wages >+0.3% MoM) would materially
increase the probability of a June rate hike or a hawkish hold signal,
pushing 10Y yields toward the 4.67% danger zone and compressing equity
multiples. The asymmetric risk is tilted bearish: a strong print = rate
hike fears + sell equities + sell bonds + buy DXY + sell Gold/Silver. A
weak print (<120K) could provide relief relief, but with inflation still
running hot at 3.8%, the Fed likely interprets weakness as complexity rather
than license to cut. Implied vol ahead of the print (VIX3M at 19.36 vs spot
15.55) suggests the market is partially hedged — but not fully.
