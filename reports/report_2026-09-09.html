XLU (Utilities, +1.77% relative 1M) and XLK (Tech, +1.86% relative 1M) are the next-best relative performers — a slightly odd pairing of defensive-yield and growth-momentum both outperforming, which typically shows up when the market is barbell-positioned against rate uncertainty. XLI (Industrials, -4.49% relative 1M) and XLV (Healthcare, -3.20% relative 5D) are the clearest laggards — Industrials likely absorbing the Canada tariff escalation, Healthcare on its own idiosyncratic weakness. Net signal: risk appetite is narrowing around energy and a defensive/quality tilt, not broad-based risk-on.

HIGHEST IMPACT EVENT: Friday's CPI print is the fulcrum of the week. Markets are unusually positioned for a hike, not a cut, at the September 16 FOMC — a regime shift from the disinflation narrative priced through most of the year. With 10Y yields already at 52-week highs (4.81%) and oil up +64% YTD feeding directly into headline and core-adjacent inflation expectations, a CPI beat above consensus risks a disorderly repricing: SPX could test SMA50 (7,598) quickly, and the 10Y could probe toward 5.00%. Conversely, a soft print would relieve the hike premium and could trigger a sharp DXY bounce (short covering off the 20D low) and unwind some of the Gold/Silver bid. The asymmetry favors having hedges in place into Friday given elevated put/call ratios already reflect some of this, but VIX term structure is still calm — meaning a surprise in either direction has outsized gap risk.
