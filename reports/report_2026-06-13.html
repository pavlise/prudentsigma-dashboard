Dominant Macro Theme Today:
Markets are being pulled in two directions by a high-stakes geopolitical
inflection point. On June 12, US and Iranian negotiators — brokered by
Pakistan — agreed on the final draft text of a peace deal that would
end active hostilities, lift oil sanctions, and reopen the Strait of
Hormuz. However, the ceasefire suffered its most serious breakdown on
June 11 when the US launched strikes across southern Iran, keeping
market confidence fragile and 82% of traders sceptical a deal closes.
Layered on top is a pivotal FOMC meeting (June 16-17) under new Chair
Kevin Warsh, with rates widely expected to hold at 3.50–3.75% but the
dot plot and Warsh's inaugural press conference tone presenting
asymmetric risk for equities.

**HIGHEST IMPACT EVENT — FOMC Decision, June 17 (Kevin Warsh's inaugural
press conference as Fed Chair):**

The June 17 FOMC meeting is the most consequential near-term catalyst.
The Fed funds rate is expected to hold at 3.50–3.75% (third consecutive
pause), but this is a non-event in terms of the rate decision itself.
The market-moving variable is the dot plot and Warsh's inaugural tone.
Warsh is historically hawkish — if the new dot plot pushes any 2026 cut
into 2027, or if Warsh signals discomfort with current financial
conditions being "too loose," the 10Y yield could re-test 4.67% and
tech multiples would face pressure (NDX has a -0.66 correlation to 10Y).
Conversely, if the dot plot holds a September cut window open, equities
could break above SMA20 resistance and trigger a short-squeeze rally.
The asymmetric risk is to the hawkish side — markets are positioned for
neutrality, not surprise hawkishness. Position accordingly: hedges into
Tuesday close, rotate into quality/defensives, reduce duration exposure.
