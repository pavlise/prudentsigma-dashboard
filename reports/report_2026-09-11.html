Markets are caught in a stagflationary vice. Brent crude spiked to $107+ this week (WTI $98.76, +8% on 5D) as the US-Iran conflict escalates around the Strait of Hormuz and the Red Sea, with Saudi output down ~1.9mb/d in August. Oil-driven inflation colliding with a hawkish Fed pivot is the single dominant cross-asset driver today.

Technical Condition Summary:
- Trend structure: SPX and NDX have both cracked below their 20D and 50D SMAs while remaining comfortably above 200D SMAs (+6.1% and +7.4% respectively) — a mid-cycle pullback, not a trend break, so far. DXY is similarly compressed between its 20/50D SMAs, unresolved. WTI is decisively bullish across all three MAs (+11.6%/+18.7%/+24.1%), the cleanest trend on the board.
- Momentum: RSI readings are unusually neutral across risk assets (SPX 44, NDX 46, BTC 54, ETH 59) — no oversold extreme yet despite the drawdown, meaning there's room for further downside before a mean-reversion bounce becomes statistically likely. WTI's RSI of 68 flags the oil rally as stretched, a tension point for next week.
- Volatility: VIX +12.5% on the week to 17.21 with the term structure still in contango (VIX3M 19.73) — the market isn't yet pricing panic, just event risk around FOMC. WTI's ATR of 4.17% and BB%B of 0.95 (near upper band) show energy vol is the most extended pocket on the board; Silver's ATR (3.02%) and proximity to its lower band flag two-way risk there.
- Cross-asset signals: SPX/HYG correlation of 0.76 is holding — no credit stress divergence yet, which is a mild positive. But WTI/HYG correlation of -0.80 means the high-yield complex is directly exposed to the energy shock (energy issuers are a meaningful chunk of HYG, and diesel/input costs squeeze other credits). Gold/DXY correlation is only -0.63, weaker than typical, suggesting gold's -1.04% 5D slide is more about real-yield pressure (10Y at 4.94%) than dollar strength — a genuine hawkish-Fed story, not just USD noise.
- Options sentiment: SPY (1.44) and QQQ (1.24) put/call volume ratios both show elevated put buying — institutional hedging is already active ahead of the FOMC decision, consistent with the technical breakdown below the 50D SMAs.

Sector Rotation: XLE (Energy) is the standout, +6.39% on 1M and +8.41% relative to SPX — a clean, high-conviction expression of the oil shock. XLK (Tech) is roughly flat relative to SPX on 1M (+0.10%) despite the NDX drawdown, meaning tech is underperforming in absolute terms but not being singled out — this is a macro/rates story, not a tech-specific de-rating. Defensives are notably NOT leading (XLP -0.32% relative, XLU -0.99% relative) — that's the tell this isn't a classic risk-off rotation into safety, it's a rates/energy shock hitting duration-sensitive and cyclical sectors broadly. XLI's -6.22% relative 1M underperformance is the biggest laggard, consistent with margin compression from higher energy input costs.

HIGHEST IMPACT EVENT: The September 16 FOMC decision is the dominant catalyst of the week. With August CPI printing 3.4% YoY headline (in line with consensus) and core still elevated near 2.4%, and energy prices accelerating into the print via the Iran conflict, markets have shifted from pricing a possible cut to pricing a live hike risk for the first time since 2023 — an unusual and asymmetric setup. Fed funds futures and the SPY/QQQ put-call skew both reflect this repricing. The asymmetry: if the Fed holds and frames the oil shock as "transitory/geopolitical," risk assets could see a relief rally given how much hedging is already in place (VIX +12.5%, elevated puts). If the Fed hikes and signals more tightening to come, the combination of a 5%+ 10Y yield and a hawkish Fed alongside $100+ oil is the kind of stagflation cocktail that historically compresses equity multiples fastest — SPX below its 50D and testing 200D support (7,157, ~6% below spot) becomes the real risk scenario.
