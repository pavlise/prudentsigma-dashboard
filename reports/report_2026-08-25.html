Fed funds futures are pricing almost no September cut (~1%) and a rising probability of a hold-or-hike stance into year-end, which explains why gold and silver — not equities — are absorbing the risk-off flow this week.

Technical Condition Summary:
- Trend structure: SPX remains in a broad bull structure (above SMA50/SMA200 by +1.4%/+7.8%) but has slipped fractionally below its SMA20 — a short-term wobble, not a trend break. NDX is weaker, now below both SMA20 and SMA50 while still comfortably above SMA200 (+8.1%) — this is early-stage tech underperformance within an intact secular uptrend, consistent with the chip-stock selloff.
- Momentum: MACD is bearish on both SPX and NDX, and RSI has cooled to 52/46 respectively — neutral, no oversold signal yet, meaning there's room to fall further before a bounce case builds. By contrast, Gold (76), BTC (84), and ETH (80) are all flashing overbought — momentum has become one-sided in hard/digital assets, raising mean-reversion risk there even as equities lack a similar extreme in either direction.
- Volatility: VIX term structure remains in healthy contango (15.85 spot vs 18.56 3M), signaling no acute panic, but ATR% on Gold (1.77%), Silver (2.68%) and BTC (2.75%) show elevated realized volatility building in the "safety trade." Equity ATR is comparatively contained (SPX 0.87%), meaning the volatility story this week is concentrated in commodities/crypto, not stocks — yet.
- Cross-asset signals: SPX/HYG correlation is strongly positive (0.84), and HYG is still grinding higher (+1.03% 1M) — credit is not confirming equity stress, an important stabilizer. Gold/DXY correlation is notably negative in this dataset (-0.47), meaning gold's rally is currently more dollar-driven than pure risk-off flight — a distinction that matters for how durable the gold trade is if DXY stabilizes.
- Options sentiment: SPY (1.34) and QQQ (1.56) put/call ratios both show elevated put buying — QQQ skew is notably more defensive than SPY, consistent with the tech-specific weakness and the binary Nvidia print risk this week.

Sector Rotation: Over the past month, Energy (+8.14%, +4.91% rel) and Healthcare (+6.92%, +3.68% rel) are the clear leaders, while Utilities (-5.39%, -8.62% rel) and Industrials (-2.29%, -5.53% rel) lag hardest. This is a classic late-cycle/defensive-value rotation: money is favoring commodity-linked and healthcare defensives over rate-sensitive Industrials/Utilities and (on the 5-day tape) even Tech, which — despite still leading over 1M — has gone flat/negative over the last week (-3.00% 5D). Institutional flows appear to be de-risking out of high-multiple growth ahead of the Nvidia/Jackson Hole binary.

HIGHEST IMPACT EVENT: Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote (Thu Aug 28) is the week's true tail-risk event. As his first appearance since taking the Fed chair in May 2026, markets have almost no read on his reaction function — fed funds futures already price just a ~1% chance of a September cut and a rising 35%→66% (Sept→Dec) probability of a hike, an unusually hawkish repricing for a "symposium" week. With 69% of surveyed fund managers expecting a neutral tone, the asymmetry favors a hawkish surprise: a break from forward guidance combined with sticky Friday PCE data (consensus +0.2% m/m core, 3.2% y/y) could reprice the curve sharply, pressuring long-duration tech (already technically weak per NDX MACD/RSI) and giving DXY a relief bounce that would cap gold's overbought rally. Conversely, any dovish signaling would likely trigger a violent unwind of the current defensive/commodity rotation back into growth.
