────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Technical Condition Summary:

- Trend structure: SPX and NDX remain in macro bull territory (above SMA50
  and SMA200) but have both rolled below their SMA20s — a near-term inflection
  that often marks the beginning of a deeper corrective leg. Gold has broken
  down through all three MAs and is in confirmed bear structure. WTI, also
  below SMA20/50 but 23% above SMA200, reflects the violent oil-price spike
  now partially unwinding post-ceasefire. BTC/ETH/SOL are in full bear
  structure — all three sit below their 20/50/200 SMAs.

- Momentum: RSI divergences are the most telling signal today. BTC (RSI 27)
  and ETH (RSI 28) are oversold at extremes — historically a near-term bounce
  trigger, though not a trend reversal in isolation. Silver (RSI 33) and Gold
  (RSI 36) are approaching oversold territory after a punishing month. SPX
  (RSI 50) and NDX (RSI 54) sit at neutral — neither providing a clear momentum
  signal, which itself is a warning: the prior up-thrust has stalled.

- Volatility: VIX at 18.92 and in contango (VIX3M @ 20.79) implies the
  market expects tension to persist but is not yet in panic. The contango
  structure means selling near-term VIX spikes has a carry advantage. Silver's
  ATR of 3.78% and WTI's 5.94% ATR indicate extraordinarily wide daily ranges —
  both require wide stops and reduced position sizing. SPX ATR at 1.05%
  suggests ~78pt daily range to factor into trade management.

- Cross-asset signals: The SPX/10Y correlation of -0.75 is the pivotal
  relationship: yields rising = equities under pressure. HYG (high yield credit)
  is performing broadly in line with equities (correlation 0.82, HYG only -0.45%
  on the week) — no credit blowup yet, which prevents a systemic read. The
  DXY/Gold correlation of -0.74 confirms the dollar is the primary driver of
  gold's current sell-off, not a sudden revival of risk appetite.

- Options sentiment: SPY P/C at 1.00 is neutral but QQQ P/C at 1.35
  reveals institutional asymmetry — funds are paying for tech downside
  protection ahead of the CPI print. This is consistent with NDX's -4.06%
  five-day underperformance and the Bearish MACD across tech.

Sector Rotation: Healthcare (XLV, +6.95% relative to SPX over 5 days) and
Consumer Staples (XLP, +4.20% relative) are absorbing institutional flows — a
textbook defensive rotation. Energy (XLE, +3.32% relative) is holding despite
crude's weekly slide, supported by still-elevated absolute oil prices. Technology
(XLK, -4.40% relative) is the clear laggard after Friday's blowout jobs data
stoked rate fears. This rotation pattern is consistent with late-cycle
stagflationary positioning, not a growth scare — investors are sheltering in
cash-generative defensives, not fleeing to bonds.
