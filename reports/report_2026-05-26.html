Technical Condition Summary:

- Trend structure: SPX and NDX remain in confirmed bull structure — both trading above SMA20, SMA50, and SMA200. SPX sits +9.8% above its 200-day MA (6,807), a historically stretched reading. BTC remains in a full bear structure, sitting below all three MAs including the SMA200 at 80,403.

- Momentum: NDX RSI at 71 is the clearest overbought signal and represents the primary equity risk. SPX RSI at 68 is elevated but not yet alarming. Gold (RSI 41), WTI (RSI 42), and Silver (RSI 48) are all in mid-range/oversold territory — setting up potential mean-reversion entries. ETH at RSI 37 is approaching oversold. Notably, there are no extreme oversold divergences in equities, limiting the probability of a sharp immediate reversal absent a macro shock.

- Volatility: VIX at 16.59 has compressed meaningfully (-8.14% 5-day), but the VIX term structure is in contango (VIX3M at 20.03 vs VIX at 16.59), meaning the market is pricing elevated volatility 3 months out — a signal that sophisticated players are hedging tail risk even as spot fear is low. WTI's ATR% at 7.17% is extreme — the oil market is in a volatility regime of its own. Silver ATR at 4.32% reflects continued precious/industrial metals repricing.

- Cross-asset signals: The SPX/HYG correlation (0.85) is holding firm — credit (HYG +0.47% 5D) is not yet diverging from equities, which is reassuring. The Gold/DXY correlation at -0.65 indicates gold remains dollar-driven; the DXY's mild +0.60% 1-month gain is explaining some of gold's -3.14% pullback. The SPX/10Y inverse correlation (-0.84) is critical ahead of Wednesday's PCE data — any upside yield shock would hit equities hard given this tight linkage.

- Options sentiment: SPY put/call at 1.26 and QQQ at 1.58 signal elevated institutional hedging. This is not panic (VIX is low) but rather deliberate portfolio insurance ahead of this week's high-impact data. The asymmetry here favors buying volatility cheaply before the PCE print.

Sector Rotation: Technology (XLK) is the dominant leader at +12.59% 1-month and +8.28% relative to SPX — driven by AI earnings beats and multiple expansion. Healthcare (XLV) is quietly emerging as a secondary leader (+3.96% 1M, -0.34% relative — nearly in-line). Energy (XLE) is the clear laggard at -1.80% 5-day (-2.75% relative) as Iran ceasefire talks hammer crude. Utilities (XLU) and Financials (XLF) are underperforming significantly on a 1-month basis (-6.10% and -3.29% relative respectively), consistent with rising rates compressing rate-sensitive sectors. Institutional money is concentrated in growth/tech — a late-cycle rotation signal worth monitoring for mean reversion.

HIGHEST IMPACT EVENT — Wednesday May 28: PCE Deflator + GDP 2nd Release

Wednesday is the most consequential session of the week. The PCE Deflator — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge — arrives at 08:30 ET alongside the Q1 GDP 2nd revision. The setup is asymmetrically dangerous: a hot PCE print (consensus ~2.6% core YoY) would validate new Fed Chair Warsh's hawkish signaling, crush expectations of any near-term rate cuts, and re-ignite bond market stress with the 10Y already at 4.56% (just 11bp from its 52-week high of 4.67%). Given that SPX/10Y carry a -0.84 30-day correlation, a yield spike would directly pressure equities — particularly the overbought NDX (RSI 71). A cool print, conversely, could catalyze a breakout through SPX 7,501 (52W high). The GDP revision is secondary but a meaningful downward revision to Q1 growth (<2.0% annualized) would add a stagflation narrative that would punish equities more than bonds. Positioning: the QQQ put/call ratio at 1.58 suggests the smart money is already hedged — consider this confirmation that Wednesday carries real tail risk.
