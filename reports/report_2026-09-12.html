Dominant Macro Theme Today: Markets are caught in a pincer between a hawkish repricing of next week's FOMC decision and an escalating US-Iran conflict in the Strait of Hormuz that has pushed WTI above $100 for the first time this cycle. Hotter-than-expected August core CPI (+0.3% m/m, ~3.4% y/y) alongside strong payrolls has flipped rate-market pricing from a comfortable "hold" toward a meaningful probability of a 25bp hike on September 16 — an outcome equity and credit markets are not yet fully discounting. Layer on Saudi Aramco's Jizan facility being struck twice in a month and tanker attacks in Hormuz, and the setup is stagflationary: rising energy costs colliding with a central bank now forced to consider tightening into slowing growth.

Technical Condition Summary:
- Trend structure: SPX and NDX both remain in intact bull structures on the higher timeframe — above SMA50 (+0.7% / +0.6%) and well above SMA200 (+6.9% / +8.3%) — but both have slipped below their SMA20s, a short-term pullback within a longer uptrend, not yet a trend break. Gold and Silver have flipped to bear structure below their SMA200s (-3.5% and -10.6% respectively), a notable divergence from the "safe haven" playbook given the geopolitical backdrop — dollar resilience and rate-hike odds are outweighing the flight-to-safety bid.
- Momentum: RSI readings are broadly neutral across equities (SPX 50, NDX 51) — no directional edge from momentum alone. The standout is WTI at RSI 72 (overbought) with BB%B at 0.99 (pinned to the upper band), signaling an extended move that is vulnerable to a sharp mean-reversion if a ceasefire headline hits. ETH's RSI 64 with BB%B 0.88 shows similar upper-band exhaustion in crypto beta.
- Volatility: VIX term structure remains in contango (15.84 spot vs 18.60 3M), the "normal" configuration, but WTI's ATR% of 4.14% and Gold/Silver ATR% north of 1.6-3% show realized volatility is already elevated in commodities well ahead of equities (SPX ATR just 0.81%). This gap typically closes into major catalysts like FOMC.
- Cross-asset signals: The SPX/HYG correlation of 0.76 remains intact — high yield credit is not yet flashing stress, a reassuring sign for equity bulls. However, WTI's correlation to SPX is -0.65 and to the 10Y is +0.72: rising oil is mechanically pressuring both risk assets and rates simultaneously, the classic stagflation transmission channel. Gold/DXY correlation at -0.63 confirms gold's weakness is dollar-driven rather than a pure risk-off unwind — if the Fed hikes and DXY firms, gold has further downside before any safe-haven bid reasserts.
- Options sentiment: SPY (1.61) and QQQ (1.72) put/call volume ratios both show elevated put buying — a bearish/hedging lean into the FOMC event risk. Open interest ratios (1.40 / 1.84) confirm this isn't just day-trading noise but positioning for a multi-day move.

Sector Rotation: Energy (XLE) is the clear leader, +2.48% relative to SPX over 5 days and +8.50% over 1 month, directly tracking the oil spike — a classic geopolitical/inflation rotation. Defensives (Healthcare, Staples, Utilities, Real Estate) are all lagging on a relative basis over both 5D and 1M, while Financials and Tech are roughly market-performers. This is a "commodity-led, defensive-light" rotation pattern — not a broad risk-off, but a narrow energy-driven reallocation that typically precedes broader volatility if the oil shock persists.

HIGHEST IMPACT EVENT: The September 15-16 FOMC decision is the dominant catalyst of the week and arguably the quarter. Consensus has shifted materially since the hot August core CPI print (+0.3% m/m, ~3.4% y/y annualized) and strong payrolls — prediction markets are split, with some venues pricing a hold near 70-75% and others pricing hike odds as high as 60-65%, reflecting genuine two-way uncertainty rather than a priced-in outcome. This is unusual: normally by the week of the meeting the market has converged on a single scenario. The asymmetry here is stark — a hold with dovish language (acknowledging energy-driven inflation as transitory) likely triggers a relief rally in equities and a pullback in the 10Y toward 4.85-4.90%, while a hike (or a hawkish hold citing above-target core inflation) would push the 10Y toward 5.10-5.15%, likely break SPX's SMA50 support at 7,607, and extend DXY's rebound off its current 3-MA-below posture. Given oil at $100 is itself inflationary, Fed Chair Warsh faces a genuine dilemma: hike into an energy shock and risk a growth scare, or hold and risk looking behind the inflation curve. Position sizing into this event should be conservative until the decision and presser are digested.
