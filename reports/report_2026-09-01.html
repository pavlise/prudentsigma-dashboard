Technical Condition Summary:

- Trend structure: SPX and NDX remain in intact bull structures on a longer horizon (both +7.5%/+8.4% above their 200-day SMAs) but have lost short-term momentum — SPX is below its 20-day SMA and NDX is below both its 20- and 50-day SMAs. This is a "healthy bull pausing" pattern, not a trend break, unless SPX loses 7,571 (50-day SMA).
- Momentum: RSI readings are notably muted for a market near record highs — SPX at 51 and NDX at 49, both essentially neutral. That's constructive in the sense there's no overbought unwind risk, but it also confirms the fade in upside conviction seen in the MACD readings, which have turned bearish on both SPX and NDX.
- Volatility: ATR% is compressed across equities (SPX 0.80%, NDX 1.41%) while WTI's ATR has expanded to 3.92% — volatility is migrating from equities into commodities, a classic pre-shock rotation. VIX's contango (15.24 spot vs. 17.76 3M) says the options market is pricing this as contained for now, which is the biggest asymmetric risk in today's setup.
- Cross-asset signals: Gold and Silver are both below their 200-day SMAs despite oil and geopolitical stress rising — the Gold/DXY correlation of -0.53 confirms this is currently a dollar-strength story (DXY +0.48% on 5D) suppressing the metals rather than a "risk-off flight to safety" bid. Should that correlation flip positive with a DXY breakdown, it would signal genuine panic building. HYG's -0.83% 5D move alongside SPX weakness (correlation 0.69) shows credit is softening in lockstep with equities — no independent divergence yet, but worth monitoring given WTI's inverse relationship to both (-0.65 to HYG, -0.62 to SPX).
- Options sentiment: SPY put/call volume at 0.95 and QQQ at 1.13 are both neutral-to-slightly-cautious, not showing a hedging rush. Elevated open interest put/call ratios (SPY 1.20, QQQ 2.08) suggest structural hedges are in place from prior weeks, but fresh flow isn't yet chasing protection despite the Hormuz headlines — a gap between the news cycle and options positioning that could close violently if oil keeps running.

Sector Rotation: Energy (XLE) is the standout leader, +9.65% over 1M and +10.65% relative to SPX — a direct read-through of the Hormuz risk premium and WTI's technical breakout. Industrials (XLI) is the clear laggard, -7.03% over 1M (-6.03% relative), likely reflecting rate and input-cost sensitivity as the 10Y pushes to 52-week highs. Healthcare (XLV) is a quiet outperformer (+5.75%/+6.74% relative), consistent with a defensive rotation beneath the surface even as headline indices hold up. This pattern — energy and defensives up, industrials and rate-sensitive cyclicals down — is a classic late-cycle, geopolitical-risk rotation rather than a broad risk-on advance.

HIGHEST IMPACT EVENT: Friday's Nonfarm Payrolls (Sep. 5) is the single most consequential print of the week, arriving with the 10Y yield already at a fresh 52-week high (4.77%) and oil breaking out on Hormuz escalation — a combination that leaves markets vulnerable to a "bad news is bad news" reaction rather than the "bad news is good news" (rate-cut hopes) dynamic that dominated earlier in the cycle. Consensus is tracking near the 150-175k range for payrolls with unemployment steady in the low-4% band. A hot print (>200k, or wage growth surprise) risks pushing the 10Y through 4.85-4.90%, which combined with the oil shock would be a genuine catalyst for the SPX to test and likely breach its 50-day SMA at 7,571. Conversely, a soft print (<100k) would let the Fed's September 16 decision lean dovish, providing equities an offset to the energy/rate headwind — watch the immediate curve reaction (2s10s) alongside the headline number.
