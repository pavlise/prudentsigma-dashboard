Technical Condition Summary:

- Trend Structure: A clean structural divergence exists between equities and everything else. SPX and NDX remain above their SMA50 and SMA200 (bull structure intact), though both are just below their SMA20 — suggesting a short-term consolidation within a larger uptrend rather than a reversal. In contrast, Gold, Silver, BTC, ETH, and EUR/USD are in confirmed bear structures: all three major moving averages stacked above price on each of these assets. WTI is the lone hybrid — bull on the SMA200 basis (+3.8%) but deeply broken below SMA20 and SMA50.

- Momentum: RSI readings tell a two-speed story. DXY at RSI 71 is the sole overbought signal — a caution flag for USD bulls entering fresh long positions. Meanwhile, WTI (RSI 31), Gold (RSI 36), Silver (RSI 36), and BTC (RSI 36) are all approaching oversold territory simultaneously, which historically signals a short-term technical bounce risk even in structural downtrends. Critically, none have reached the 30 threshold that defines capitulatory oversold, so momentum shorts remain viable for now.

- Volatility: VIX at 18.44 is elevated versus its 52W low of 13.47 but well off the 31.05 high — a regime consistent with late-cycle consolidation rather than acute stress. The VIX term structure in contango (spot 16.78 vs. 3M at 19.57) suggests the market is hedging for future uncertainty, not pricing panic today. Silver's ATR of 4.0% and WTI's ATR of 6.79% flag these as high-volatility instruments requiring wide stops. SPX ATR of 1.34% is well-behaved.

- Cross-Asset Signals: The SPX/HYG correlation at +0.82 and HYG holding flat on the week (+0.09% 5D) is constructive — credit is not signalling stress ahead of equity weakness, a key leading indicator. The DXY/Gold correlation (negative, consistent with long-run history) is back in force: dollar strength is the primary mechanism of gold's 8.1% monthly drawdown. Notably, the 30-day SPX/BTC correlation has compressed to just +0.52, suggesting crypto is following its own bear narrative independent of equity momentum.

- Options Sentiment: SPY put/call volume at 1.12 is neutral, but QQQ's put/call volume ratio of 1.52 with open interest at 3.12 reveals meaningful tail-risk hedging in tech specifically. This is not a conviction short signal but suggests institutional desks are protecting NDX gains — consistent with the just-below-SMA20 consolidation pattern.

Sector Rotation: XLK (Technology) is the undisputed leader, +7.17% vs. SPX over the past month, driven by NVDA, MSFT, META, and GOOGL all delivering massive Q1 beats. XLI (Industrials, +5.06% rel.) and XLF (Financials, +2.79% rel.) are secondary leaders. XLE (Energy) is the critical laggard at -10.99% relative to SPX over 1 month — the Iran peace deal has devastated the sector. XLP (Staples, -3.70% rel.) and XLV (Healthcare, +0.64% rel.) signal muted defensive appetite, further corroborating the risk-on equity thesis.

The Core PCE is the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge, and Thursday's release lands at a uniquely dangerous junction. With 9 FOMC members already on record supporting another rate hike and the DXY at a 52-week high reflecting this repricing, the Core PCE print is binary:

- Hot print (>2.8% YoY): Confirms the hawkish pivot narrative. DXY extends above 101.5, 10Y yield tests 4.55–4.67% (52W high), gold accelerates below 4,090, SPX tests SMA50 at 7,301. *Asymmetric downside for risk assets.*
- In-line/cool print (≤2.6% YoY): Partial relief. Rate hike bets partially unwind, DXY pullback from overbought RSI 71 creates a reflexive gold bounce toward 4,350 (SMA20). SPX reclaims SMA20 at 7,482. *Short covering rally in beaten-down assets.*

Market positioning is currently skewed for the hot scenario (DXY at 52W high, commodity selloff already underway), meaning a cool print carries the more explosive upside surprise potential for gold and equities alike. Size risk accordingly.
