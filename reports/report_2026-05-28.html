SECTION 1 — MACRO PULSE (5-MINUTE READ)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Dominant Macro Theme Today:
The defining tension of this market is the deepening equity-bond divergence
playing out against the backdrop of the US-Iran war and its evolving peace
negotiations. Equity indices sit at all-time highs — driven by an
extraordinary AI earnings-beat cycle — while the bond market screams fiscal
stress: the 30-year Treasury touched 5.2%, a 19-year high, before retreating,
and the 10Y yield is up 7% YTD. The partial peace signal from Iran's updated
proposal (delivered via Pakistani mediators) is hammering crude — WTI is
down 15.6% in a month — yet equity markets are decoupled, treating falling
oil as a pure demand/margin windfall for corporates rather than a signal of
geopolitical de-risking.

Conviction Level: Medium
Reason: Equities are overbought on RSI (SPX 71, NDX 74) with bearish MACD

SECTION 2 — TECHNICAL SNAPSHOT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Technical Condition Summary:

- Trend structure: Equities are in a clean bull structure — SPX and NDX
  trade 7-12% above their 50-day SMAs and 10-18% above the 200-day. This is
  not a tentative breakout; this is a sustained, institutionally-backed
  momentum regime. In contrast, WTI Crude (-8%/-9% below SMA20/50) and BTC
  (-5%/-8.5% below SMA50/200) are in confirmed bear structures. Gold sits in
  a neutral zone: below the 20 and 50-day SMAs but still 1.3% above the
  200-day — its last structural support line at 4,364.

- Momentum: The RSI picture is telling a two-sided story. SPX at 71 and
  NDX at 74 are firmly in overbought territory — not extreme enough to call
  a top, but sufficient to demand caution on new longs at current prices.
  On the other side, ETH is at RSI 29 (oversold), BTC at 35, Gold at 34,
  and WTI at 41 — these are mean-reversion candidates, particularly ETH
  and Gold where oversold readings are supported by proximity to structural
  support. The RSI divergence between equities and commodities/crypto is
  historically unusual and represents a regime of extreme sector rotation
  rather than broad-based risk appetite.

- Volatility: VIX at 16.67 in contango (VIX3M at 19.45) signals that
  the options market is not pricing near-term stress but does expect
  volatility to rise over the next 3 months — likely surrounding the June
  16-17 FOMC meeting and NFP data. SPX ATR is modest at 0.96% while WTI
  ATR is 7.06% — crude is the live grenade in cross-asset terms right now.
  BB%B on equities (SPX 0.83, NDX 0.84) signals prices are running near
  the upper Bollinger Band — consistent with overbought/extended readings.

- Cross-asset signals: The SPX/HYG correlation at 0.83 (30-day) with
  HYG posting modest gains (+0.19% in a month) shows credit markets are NOT
  yet sending a divergence warning — this is constructive for equities.
  However, the Gold/DXY negative correlation (-0.64) while the DXY is
  strengthening technically is a headwind for gold bulls. The SPX/WTI
  negative correlation (-0.65) is the key relationship to watch: if the
  Iran deal collapses and oil spikes, this correlation could become the
  primary equity risk channel.

- Options sentiment: SPY P/C ratio at 0.95 and QQQ at 0.97 — both
  firmly neutral. The market is not hedging aggressively, consistent with
  the low VIX. This is a complacent positioning setup: historically, when
  P/C ratios are near 1.0 and VIX is sub-17, sharp drawdowns are often
  preceded by exactly this kind of options-market quiet.

Sector Rotation: Technology (XLK) is the unambiguous leader — up 16.84%
in the past month vs. SPX +5.34%, a +11.49% relative outperformance. This
reflects the AI earnings supercycle (META beat by $3.78, GOOGL by $2.44,
NVDA by $0.10 on a forward P/E of just 16.8x). Energy (XLE) is the
laggard, -1.25% in a month vs. SPX +5.34% (-6.59% relative), confirming
the crude oil selloff as Iran negotiations advance. Financials and Utilities
are also lagging — consistent with a rising-rate, tech-driven Risk-On regime
rather than a broad market advance. Institutional money is concentrated in
megacap tech; breadth risk remains elevated.

HIGHEST IMPACT EVENT — Core PCE (May 29):
April Core PCE is the single most important release of the week — and
arguably of the month. The Fed faces its sharpest trade-off in years:
energy prices are up ~57% YTD even after their recent pullback, raising
the risk that pass-through inflation is more persistent than the equity
market has priced. The bond market already demands compensation: 30Y yields
hit 19-year highs earlier this month at 5.2%. A hot Core PCE print (above
+0.3% MoM or +2.8% YoY) would validate the bond market's thesis, push the
10Y back toward 4.67% (52W high), and likely trigger a sharp tech
de-rating given NDX trades at 74 RSI and near its ATH. Conversely, an
in-line or soft print (+0.2% MoM) would confirm the "immaculate
disinflation" narrative and could propel NDX through 30,000 with conviction.
The asymmetry is notable: upside in equities from a soft print is probably
1.5-2%, while a hot print could trigger a 3-4% drawdown in NDX. Position
defensively into the number.
