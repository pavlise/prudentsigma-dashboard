## SECTION 2 — TECHNICAL SNAPSHOT

Technical Condition Summary:
- Trend structure: SPX is in a clean bull structure — above SMA20 (7,481), SMA50 (7,472), and SMA200 (7,024), with the 200-day nearly 470 points below spot, confirming a durable uptrend. NDX is more fragile: it's below both its SMA20 and SMA50 (a short-term correction inside a longer-term uptrend), signaling mega-cap tech has underperformed the broader index over the past month (-3.6% vs SPX +0.09%).
- Momentum: RSI readings are unremarkable and non-extreme across the board — SPX at 53, NDX at 45, Gold at 50, WTI at 55 — meaning no asset is flashing an overbought/oversold reversal signal. MACD is bearish on SPX, NDX, DXY, and BTC even as price grinds higher, a classic momentum-lagging-price divergence worth watching for a shallow pullback.
- Volatility: VIX at 15.99 with VIX3M at 19.02 keeps the term structure in healthy contango (normal risk regime), but the 14% weekly VIX collapse alongside SPX's modest 1% gain suggests options hedges are being unwound aggressively — a classic setup for complacency if a fresh catalyst hits. WTI's ATR% of 5.44% is by far the widest in the book, reflecting the ongoing tariff-driven crude repricing.
- Cross-asset signals: SPX/VIX correlation sits at -0.94 (textbook fear-gauge behavior, no divergence). SPX/HYG correlation is a solid +0.78 with HYG's 5-day return only +0.26% — credit is not confirming the equity rally with the same enthusiasm, an early yellow flag rather than a red one. Gold/DXY correlation of -0.52 confirms gold's bid is currently dollar-driven, not a pure flight-to-safety signal.
- Options sentiment: SPY and QQQ put/call ratios (1.03 and 1.02 on volume) are both neutral, showing no strong directional skew from options flow. Elevated open-interest P/C ratios (SPY 1.27, QQQ 1.75) versus lower volume ratios suggest existing hedges are being held/rolled rather than aggressively added — consistent with a market that's calm but not complacent-euphoric yet.

Sector Rotation: Energy (XLE) is the standout leader, +11.81% relative to SPX over the past month on the back of WTI's surge — a genuine cyclical/inflation trade, not just a bounce. Financials (XLF, +2.29% relative) are also outperforming, aided by the strong JPM/GS earnings beats and the steepening curve. Defensives and rate-sensitives are broadly lagging: Utilities (XLU, -3.17% relative) and Industrials (XLI, -2.30% relative) are the weakest, consistent with the 10Y yield sitting at a 52-week high pressuring bond-proxy sectors. Tech (XLK, -2.99% relative) underperforming alongside NDX's technical weakness confirms this is a genuine rotation out of growth/duration and into cyclicals — a mid-cycle, reflationary tilt.

With 10Y yields already at a 52-week high (4.74%) and MACD flashing bearish divergence on SPX despite price strength, a soft services print (sub-50, contraction) would compound rate-cut repricing risk and could trigger the pullback toward the SMA50 (7,472) that current momentum indicators are already hinting at. A beat (>52) would likely extend the current risk-on grind and validate the Financials/Energy rotation thesis.
