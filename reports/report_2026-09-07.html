Contango in the VIX term structure (15.06 spot vs 17.61 3M) confirms the market is pricing this as contained rather than systemic — a setup ripe for a volatility repricing if Hormuz shipping is actually disrupted. WTI's ATR (3.83%) and Gold's ATR (1.79%) are already elevated versus the index ATRs (SPX 0.81%), showing commodity markets are the ones actively pricing risk right now.

SPX/VIX correlation sits at the normal -0.66, no divergence yet.

Sector Rotation: Energy (XLE, +11.4% 1M, +11.9% relative to SPX) is the standout leader, directly tracking the oil supply-risk narrative. Financials (+1.4% relative) and Healthcare (+4.0% relative) are outperforming defensively, while Industrials (-4.9% relative) and Real Estate (-1.8% relative) lag — a rotation pattern consistent with markets pricing in higher-for-longer rates (10Y near 52W highs) rather than outright risk-off, since classic defensives (Staples, Utilities) are not leading.

HIGHEST IMPACT EVENT: Friday's CPI report (Sep 11) is the key print this week. With the 10Y yield already pressing its 52-week high (4.78 vs 4.80 high) and WTI up nearly 60% YTD on the back of the Hormuz conflict, energy-driven base effects raise real risk of a hotter-than-expected headline CPI. A hot print would compress the odds of a rate cut at the Sep 16 FOMC meeting, likely pressuring both equities (via multiple compression, NDX/SPX are trading near cycle highs with RSI in neutral territory and room to fall) and adding further upward pressure to yields — a scenario the elevated SPY put/call ratio (1.47) suggests is already being partially hedged. Conversely, an in-line or soft core print, paired with any de-escalation headline out of the Strait of Hormuz, could unlock a sharp risk rally given how much dry powder sits on the sidelines (VIX complacency, contango structure).
