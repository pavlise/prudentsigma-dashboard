Dominant Macro Theme Today:
Markets are caught between a hawkish labor-market surprise and an escalating Middle East supply shock. August nonfarm payrolls printed at 162K versus a 53K consensus, pushing implied odds of a September Fed hike to roughly 58–65% and putting Chair Warsh's inflation-hawk stance in direct tension with VP Vance's public call for cuts and Governor Waller's more dovish tone. Simultaneously, renewed military activity around the Strait of Hormuz has lifted crude to fresh 52-week highs and pushed diesel to a record, reinforcing the inflation impulse the Fed is trying to fight. The FOMC quiet period began Sept 5 ahead of the Sept 15–16 meeting, so this week's PPI (Wed) and CPI (Fri) prints are the last hard data points before the decision — and with WTI +59.6% YTD, the inflation math is getting harder, not easier.

Gold/DXY correlation is -0.59 (dollar-driven, not pure flight-to-safety) — but Gold/Silver at +0.86 and both testing SMA200 from below together suggests a genuine, if early, precious-metals base-building attempt rather than a one-off dollar squeeze.

Sector Rotation: Energy (XLE) is the standout leader, +11.41% 1M and +11.91% relative to SPX — a direct read-through of the crude rally and confirmation that the oil move is being taken seriously by equity allocators, not just a futures-curve story. Utilities (XLU) are also outperforming on the week (+2.01%, +1.59% relative), a defensive tell alongside the SPY put/call skew. Industrials (XLI) are the clear laggard (-5.35% 1M, -4.85% relative), suggesting the market is discounting higher input costs (oil, copper +18.48% YTD) hitting margins in cyclical/industrial names. This is a rotation profile consistent with "late-cycle, inflation-anxious" positioning rather than a broad risk-off.

HIGHEST IMPACT EVENT: Thursday's CPI print (Sep 11) is the single most important data point before the Sept 15–16 FOMC decision, which is now trading with 58–65% implied odds of a hike per futures markets — an unusually high probability for a meeting that just weeks ago was priced as a hold/cut debate. Consensus will likely sit near +0.3% m/m core, but the setup is asymmetric: with oil already +11.4% on the month and diesel at record highs, a hot CPI (>0.3% core) would validate Chair Warsh's hawkish read and could push the 10Y through its 4.80 52-week high, triggering a rapid equity de-rating given SPX's already-stretched valuation multiples on the fundamentals table (AAPL 36.6x, GOOGL 17x, NVDA 29.2x). Conversely, a soft print (≤0.2% core) would validate Governor Waller's dovish camp, likely triggering a relief rally in duration-sensitive assets (Gold, growth equities, TLT) and a fresh leg down in DXY toward its 98.8 20-day low. Given the internal Fed split (Warsh vs. Waller vs. political pressure from Vance) and the FOMC quiet period muting any verbal course-correction this week, Thursday's number effectively decides the tone heading into the meeting — position sizing into this print should be conservative regardless of directional view.
