Technical Condition Summary:
- Trend structure: NDX shows the cleanest bull structure — above SMA20/50/200 with bullish MACD. SPX is in a shallower uptrend, above SMA50/200 but stalling just below SMA20 (7,658.89), a near-term pause rather than a reversal. Gold has flipped bearish (below SMA20 and SMA200, above SMA50 only) — a mixed, unconvincing structure. Silver is caught between timeframes: bullish short-term (above SMA20/50) but still below its SMA200, meaning the 5-day rally is a counter-trend bounce, not a trend change. WTI remains structurally bullish (above all three MAs) despite the sharp 5-day pullback — this reads as digestion of the earlier geopolitical spike, not trend failure.
- Momentum: RSI readings are broadly neutral (SPX 51, NDX 56, Gold 48, Silver 55, WTI 52) — no extreme overbought/oversold conditions in core assets. The exception is crypto: BTC RSI 65 and ETH RSI 67, with ETH's BB%B at 1.09 (trading above its upper band) — momentum is stretched and vulnerable to a sharp mean-reversion move.
- Volatility: VIX at 14.81 with the term structure in normal contango (VIX3M 18.24) signals no near-term stress priced into equities — arguably complacent given the weekend's Middle East headlines. ATR% tells a different story in commodities: WTI (4.71%) and Silver (2.51%) are running hot, reflecting real two-way volatility even while equity vol stays subdued.
- Cross-asset signals: SPX/VIX correlation holds at -0.86, the normal fear-gauge relationship — no divergence warning. SPX/HYG at +0.65 with no breakdown confirms credit is not flashing stress ahead of equities. The more interesting signal is Gold/DXY at -0.57: gold's weakness is dollar-driven, not risk-off — if this were a genuine flight-to-safety episode tied to the Middle East, we'd expect gold and DXY to move together (positive correlation), which they are not.
- Options sentiment: SPY put/call at 1.19 shows a modest defensive hedging tilt (elevated put demand) without being alarmed; QQQ put/call at 1.03 is balanced — consistent with tech being the market's preferred long exposure right now.

Sector Rotation: XLK is the clear leader (+2.89% 5D, +2.49% relative to SPX), consistent with NDX's outperformance and confirming the tech-led rally is real, not just an index-weighting artifact. On the other end, XLU (-1.72%/-2.12% rel) and XLRE (-1.37%/-1.77% rel) are the weakest — classic duration-sensitive laggards given the 10Y sitting at a 52-week high. XLF's -2.05%/-2.45% relative underperformance is notable given strong bank earnings (JPM, GS both beat handily) — the sector is being punished by rate-path uncertainty rather than fundamentals, a divergence worth monitoring for a reversion trade. Net signal: this is a narrow, growth-over-value, rate-averse rotation — not broad-based risk appetite.

A miss would flip the script: it would validate the recent labor/inflation softening, pressure yields lower, and likely trigger a rotation back into duration-sensitive laggards and a relief bounce in gold. With Fed speakers bracketing the print on both sides (10+ appearances this week), expect elevated rate-vol into and out of Wednesday's release — asymmetric risk favors positioning with defined stops rather than pressing directional bets pre-print.
